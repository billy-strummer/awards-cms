Year 1 Financial Plan

What this could realistically make in Year 1, going purely off nominee volume across all categories and locations — plus the three packages we'd actually sell to get there.

Recommended Year 1 planning range
£70,000 – £122,000
Conservative-to-central engagement, no ceremony run · Full range across scenarios: £70K–£184K

01 The Short Answer

Realistically, somewhere between £70,000 and £122,000 in Year 1, built entirely bottom-up from how many businesses genuinely exist per category per location, and how many of them we could actually get to respond to a brand nobody's heard of yet. No ceremony, no events, no external costs assumed — just the two of us selling three straightforward packages plus a handful of smaller revenue lines.

This isn't an investor pitch number. It's the number we'd want to see before deciding how much time to put into this, so it's built to be defensible rather than impressive — every figure below is either a real price we found operating in the market today, or a clearly labelled judgment call we made ourselves.

02 How This Number Is Built

Two separate questions, multiplied together, for every one of the 6,200 category-location combinations (100 locations × 62 categories):

QuestionAnswer
Do enough real businesses in this trade exist in this location to fill the category at all?54.5% central — bottom-up, category-by-category (see Section 3).
Of those that exist, how many would actually respond to a brand-new scheme reaching out for the first time?10–20% central range — genuine judgment; no comparable programme was ever new in the way this launch will be.

Multiplying the two gives an effective winner count of 338 (conservative) / 507 (central) / 676 (upper) out of 6,200 possible slots. Everything downstream — package sales, finalist sales, sponsorship — scales off that winner count.

08 What This Doesn't Cover

  • Our own time and any small running costs — this is a revenue figure only, not take-home profit. Worth keeping a simple running note of anything we actually spend as we go.
  • Directory, membership, or pay-per-lead revenue — all of these need a credible pool of past winners and real search traffic first. Not a Year 1 or Year 2 line.
  • Year 2 and beyond — this is scoped to Year 1 only. If the acceptance fee or logo licence renews annually, Year 2 compounds on top of new Year 1 winners rather than starting from zero, but that's not modelled here.

09 How We'd Actually Hit This Number

Since who responds, not how many categories exist, is what actually drives the number, the highest-leverage work is outreach, not more modelling. Six ways to do that without spending on ads:

  1. Consumer-driven nomination, not cold outreach to businesses. Ask homeowners to nominate their favourite builder rather than approaching tradespeople cold — a much warmer, positively-framed way in.
  2. Seed with a handful of respected local names first. A few well-regarded founding nominees per launch area lend credibility to everyone who follows.
  3. Distribute through partners who already have the audience. A builders' merchant or trade supplier sponsor already has a mailing list we don't — make early sponsorship partly about co-promotion, not just a logo fee.
  4. Local press, not paid advertising. Regional papers cover local business award stories as genuine local-interest content, at no media cost.
  5. Concentrate the launch instead of spreading thin. Fewer locations and the ~22 high-fill categories first builds real word-of-mouth, rather than diluting effort everywhere at once.
  6. Lead every message with "free to enter." The biggest blocker to engagement is "why would I bother" — make the zero-cost, zero-downside nature of entry the first thing anyone hears.

10 Before Committing Fully

The Year-1 engagement rate (Section 2) is the single biggest assumption in this whole plan, and it's the one no amount of desk research can resolve.

  • Run a small real test — pick 2–3 categories in 2–3 locations, run the actual nomination process from Section 9, and see what response rate we really get.
  • A real 20-business test beats more modelling — it replaces the single biggest guess in this report with an actual number.
  • Then revisit the package split in Section 4 against real sales, since that's the second-biggest judgment call in the plan.
Note: this is a planning estimate for our own use, not a forecast or guarantee. Section 2 (engagement rate) and Section 4 (package split) are the two numbers most worth validating against real activity before relying on this range for any bigger decision.
