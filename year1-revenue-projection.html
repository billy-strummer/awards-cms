01 Executive Summary

This report supersedes the earlier v1 estimate (£1.34M central). That figure used an unvalidated 65% category fill rate and pricing assumptions with no supporting evidence. Following a full audit — including research into real, currently-operating UK comparables — the fill rate, pricing, and event structure have all been rebuilt from evidence where evidence exists, and clearly flagged as judgment where it doesn't.

Revised central Year 1 estimate: approximately £713,000 to £923,000, depending on one major strategic decision this report identifies but does not make for you: whether to run ticketed regional ceremonies at all. The full range across all fill-rate and structural scenarios modelled is £461,000 (low) to £1,107,000 (high).

This is materially lower than the earlier estimate, for defensible reasons detailed throughout — primarily a more conservative, evidence-based category fill rate, and the discovery that the closest real comparable in the market does not run award ceremonies at all.

02 What Changed, and Why

The previous version used a category fill rate of 65% with no stated justification beyond "a reasonable central estimate." When challenged, that figure did not hold up. This version replaces it with a bottom-up, category-by-category analysis, and researches real, currently-operating UK businesses running comparable programmes — using their actual published pricing and structural choices as evidence wherever they exist, rather than continuing to guess.

04 Category Fill Rate — Bottom-Up Methodology

Every one of the 62 categories was classified by how common the trade genuinely is, then split by location density — a niche trade behaves very differently in a rural county versus a major city.

TierCatsCounty fillUrban fillExample
High-fill2280%95%Electrical, Plumbing
Medium-fill1540%80%Drainage, Pest Control
Low-fill (niche)1915%50%Structural Steelworks
Leadership635%60%Apprentice of the Year

Blended result: county-like locations (70 of 100) blend to ~46% fill; dense urban locations (30 of 100) blend to ~74%. Weighted across the full mix: central estimate 54.5%, working range 35–65%.

Location count note: the supplied figures (42+15+15+16+12) sum to 100, not the previously stated 110. This report uses 100 throughout.

05 The Structural Decision This Report Cannot Make For You

The single most important finding in this audit. The original model assumed 11 independent ticketed regional galas, generating roughly a third of total projected revenue — a structure with no support from either real comparable found:

  • Business Awards UK runs no ceremony at all, and says explicitly their own winners told them it wasn't worth it.
  • FMB — the most established trade-specific body — runs one national final, not many regional galas, and only every two years.

Three structures are modelled below at all three fill-rate scenarios. This report deliberately does not recommend one over the others — that's a brand and risk-appetite decision, not a modelling question.

06 Two Further Risks Investors Will Likely Raise

Category granularity versus market precedent

62 categories per location is considerably more granular than either comparable found. Business Awards UK runs ~4–8 categories per sector; FMB uses broader project-based categories rather than narrow trade splits. This granularity may be a genuine differentiator — but it's untested at this depth, and worth having a direct answer ready for.

Annual cadence versus market precedent

FMB's flagship awards run every two years, not annually. Even an 80+ year old, well-resourced trade body doesn't attempt full national coverage every single year. That doesn't make an annual cadence wrong — but it's a comparator worth anticipating.

09 What This Still Excludes

  • Entry fees — £0, confirmed free-to-enter model.
  • Directory, membership, pay-per-lead revenue — excluded; depends on a track record Year 1 won't have.
  • All operating costs — judging, events, sponsor sales all carry real costs not modelled here. This is a revenue projection, not a P&L.

10 Recommendations Before an Investor Meeting

  • Decide the event structure question explicitly rather than defaulting to Structure A — be ready to explain why, given the evidence.
  • Consider a reduced-scope pilot in fewer locations and the 22 high-fill categories first, to replace tier assumptions with real data before scaling to all 100 locations.
  • Have a direct answer ready on category-granularity and annual-cadence — investors who look at this market briefly will find the same comparables this audit found.
