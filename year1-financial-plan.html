01 The Short Answer

Recommended Year 1 planning range: £51,000–£103,000. This updates the previous working plan with two real pieces of information from the live site — the actual Bronze/Gold/Platinum sponsorship structure (replacing a flat £5,000-per-sponsor guess with realistic tiered pricing), and the brewery-hosted winner celebrations, which resolve the "events cost too much" objection that previously kept events out of the plan entirely, since these cost nothing to run.

The full range across all three scenarios is £51,000–£172,000. This is slightly lower at the conservative end than the previous plan, and that's a genuine improvement in accuracy, not a mistake — a flat £5,000-per-sponsor assumption overstated what's realistic when most Year 1 sponsors will sensibly start at the cheaper Bronze tier, not jump straight to a Title Partnership with a brand-new, unproven programme.

On the £50,000-each target specifically: close, but not comfortably guaranteed in Year 1 — even the central scenario falls about £2,400 short each once real running costs (never subtracted before this version) are properly accounted for. Year 2 clears it comfortably, purely from renewals. Full working in Section 9.

02 What's Changed From the Last Version

Real sponsorship tiers, not a flat guess. The site now has a proper Bronze/Gold/Platinum structure with real feature differentiation. Section 5 replaces the flat sponsorship line with pricing built from that actual structure.

The brewery celebrations resolve the events question. The previous plan deliberately excluded any event, since running one "costs real money and organisational effort." A brewery hosting for free, in exchange for the drink sales, removes that objection — Section 6 covers this properly.

The public vote mechanic doesn't change the underlying maths. The live site confirms entry is followed by vetting, then a public vote decides the winner. This is already consistent with how the model works — one winner per category, everyone else becomes a finalist — but it does mean genuine public engagement (not just getting businesses to enter) matters for a category to feel legitimate. Folded into the marketing thinking already in this plan.

A word on "65+ counties" and "1,000+ winners" on the site. The homepage and sponsor page use rounder, more aspirational figures than the precise 100-location, 62-category model this plan is built on — normal for public-facing marketing copy, but worth knowing the two aren't meant to be read as the same number. This plan uses the precise figures throughout.

03 How the Winner Count Is Built (Unchanged)

Two separate questions, multiplied together, across all 6,200 category-location combinations (100 locations × 62 categories):

  • Do enough real businesses in this trade exist in this location to fill the category at all? 54.5% central — bottom-up, category-by-category.
  • Of those that exist, how many would actually respond to a brand-new scheme reaching out for the first time? 10–20% central range — genuine judgment.

Multiplying the two gives 338 (conservative) / 507 (central) / 676 (upper) winners out of 6,200 possible slots — unchanged from the previous plan, since neither of these two questions has new evidence to revise them.

06 The Brewery Celebrations — Recommended As the Default

Ten small, private, winner-only celebrations, one per region, hosted free by a local brewery in exchange for the drink sales on the night. This is worth adopting as the default Year 1 event structure, not an optional extra:

Zero cost to run. No venue hire, and the brewery's own incentive (drink sales) means there's no reason to expect a hosting fee either. This is the one way to get a genuine in-person event without the cost objection that previously ruled events out entirely.

A real reason to be in the room for the upsell conversation. The three packages (Section 4) are easier to sell face to face, in person, to someone who's just been celebrated, than by email. No specific uplift is assumed in the figures above, since there's no evidence to size it by — but it's a reasonable expectation worth testing.

Ten separate local press opportunities, not one national one. A small regional celebration is a natural local-news story in a way a single national final isn't — potentially better organic PR reach than the more expensive single-final option considered previously, at zero cost instead of an estimated £30–50K.

Worth formalising the brewery's own role. Rather than treating each brewery purely as a free venue, consider offering them Bronze-tier sponsor recognition (Section 5) in exchange for hosting — they're already getting exposure to a room of local business owners, so making the exchange explicit costs nothing extra and turns a favour into a proper partnership.

07 Best Products to Sell — In Priority Order

Given everything above, here's where to actually spend selling effort, not just what's theoretically available:

  1. The three winner packages — sell hard, sell first. Highest volume, most predictable, real evidenced pricing. Every winner is a live conversation waiting to happen the moment they're announced.
  2. Gold sponsorship — the sweet spot, not Platinum. The site itself markets Gold as "Most Popular" for good reason — real value (category ownership, trophy, press, interview) at a price a first-time sponsor can actually say yes to. Prioritise this over chasing a small number of hard-to-close Platinum deals in Year 1.
  3. Bronze sponsorship — volume and credibility. Low friction, low individual value, but each one adds a recognisable logo to the sponsors page — useful for making the programme look more established to the next sponsor conversation, not just for the revenue itself.
  4. Finalist packages — low effort, already-warm audience. These are people who've already engaged once; a cheap, simple upsell rather than a new sales conversation.
  5. Brewery/loyalty partnerships — start now, expect little revenue yet. Worth setting up in Year 1 for the relationship and the free events, but genuinely not worth counting on for meaningful revenue until the audience has scale.

What They're Actually Renewing

This didn't have a real answer until now — earlier drafts assumed "the acceptance fee or brand licence renews annually" without saying why a past winner would pay again for the same static badge. There's nothing on the live site today that says the licence expires, so as it stands there's no honest hook to charge a winner twice. That has to be designed properly before this is a real revenue line, not just a spreadsheet assumption.

The recommended product: an annual re-verification, not a re-bill. State plainly in the Terms & Conditions that the winner brand licence is valid for 12 months. Renewal is a light-touch re-check — still trading, still insured, still holds the accreditations they entered with — not a full re-entry. In exchange, the badge refreshes to show current status (e.g. "Verified [year]") rather than sitting as a static, ageing "2026 Winner." An unrenewed badge should visibly lapse or read as historic only, versus a renewed one that reads as current — that's the actual reason to pay again, not goodwill.

Why this version, not a flat re-bill: it mirrors how real trade-body memberships already work (Federation of Master Builders' recurring membership ties to standing, not a static logo), so it's grounded in the closest real comparable rather than invented. Worth bundling with the loyalty/discount perks (Sections 5–6) so it reads as an ongoing membership, not a repeat bill for nothing new — and once a few years of winners exist, this is also the natural gateway into a directory/listings product (a renewed, verified winner is exactly who'd want a live, findable profile), which earlier drafts correctly flagged as not viable until a credible pool of past winners exists.

Before this is real, not just modelled: the T&Cs need to state the licence has a 12-month term (they currently don't), and the re-verification process needs to actually exist — what gets checked, how, and what "lapsed" looks like publicly. Until both of those are built, treat every renewal figure below as illustrative of the mechanism, not a confirmed revenue line.

10 Full Site Check — What Else Is There, and What It Changes

Checked every page on the live site (FAQs, About, the entry form, and the voting flow), not just the ones this plan was originally built from. Four things worth knowing:

Winners are selected two ways, not one. The FAQ confirms most categories are decided by public vote, but "winners in judged categories may be selected by an independent panel." Doesn't change the revenue model — one winner per active slot either way — but worth describing accurately rather than only mentioning the vote.

There's a four-tier status ladder already live: Nominee, Finalist, Top 3, and Winner. Each can carry its own branded "shield" once a brand licence is issued. This plan currently only monetises two of the four tiers (Winner via the three packages, Finalist via the finalist package) — a Top 3 tier, priced between the two, is a genuine product opportunity worth considering once the core two are selling, not a Year 1 must-have.

The FAQ doesn't yet disclose that the brand licence costs anything. It confirms winners "can promote their achievement... once British Trade Awards has issued you with an official brand licence," but nowhere states there's a fee attached — worth updating before real winners start asking, since right now a reasonable reader could assume it's free, given entry itself is repeatedly described as free.

The real entry form supports keeping engagement estimates conservative, not the opposite. It's a genuine seven-step form — location, sector, category, company details, a written business description, trade body memberships, review — plus vetting evidence (insurance, ID, trading address, qualifications) if requested. This is real friction, not a one-click nomination, which is exactly why the 10–20% engagement range (Section 3) stays conservative rather than being revised upward.

One date correction for precision: the site's own Key Dates run entries Feb–Apr 2027 and winners announced Nov 2027, even though the programme is described as "launching in 2026." Worth using the real 2027 dates consistently, since "Year 1" here means this specific cycle, not calendar year 2026 itself.

11 Getting the Engagement Rate Right — Still the Biggest Unknown

Everything above still rests on the same two numbers flagged before: the 54.5% market-existence figure (reasonably solid) and the 10–20% engagement range (genuine judgment, not evidence). Nothing about the new sponsorship structure or the brewery events changes that — it remains the single biggest thing worth testing with real activity before relying on any of these figures for a bigger decision.

  • Run a small real test — 2–3 categories in 2–3 locations, run the real nomination process, see what response rate actually comes back.
  • Revisit the package split and sponsorship tiers against real sales once the first cohort of winners exists — both are judgment calls worth correcting with real data as soon as it's available.
