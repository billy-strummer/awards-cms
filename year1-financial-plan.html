01 Executive Summary

Revised central Year 1 estimate: approximately £140,000 to £200,000. This report supersedes all earlier versions. Previous drafts (£1.34M, then £713K–£923K) correctly improved the category-by-category market analysis but never separately tested whether businesses would actually respond to a brand nobody has heard of yet — the single biggest driver of realistic Year 1 revenue. This version treats that as the central question it is, builds the plan around a conservative, defensible answer, and sets out concretely how to grow beyond it.

This is not a smaller, less ambitious version of the same plan — it is a more accurate one. A number that looks impressive but assumes near-total first-year engagement from a brand-new, unknown scheme is not a useful planning figure, however good it looks in a pitch deck. The range in this report is defensible; the marketing plan in Section 7 is what closes the gap to the larger, previously-reported figures over time.

02 Methodology — Two Factors, Not One

Every revenue figure in this report is built from two genuinely separate factors, multiplied together:

FactorQuestion It AnswersEstimate
1. Market existenceDo enough real, credible businesses in this trade exist in this location to fill the category at all?54.5% central — bottom-up, category-by-category, reasonably evidenced.
2. Year-1 engagementOf those that exist, what share would actually respond to a brand-new, unknown scheme's outreach in its first year?10–20% central range — genuine judgment; no comparable was ever new in the way this launch will be.

True effective fill rate = Factor 1 × Factor 2. At the central case (54.5% × 15%), that's approximately 8.2% of all 6,200 category-location slots nationally — a materially different starting point than treating market existence alone as the answer.

Why Factor 2 is judgment, not evidence: neither comparable found in this research (Business Awards UK, FMB) publishes first-year participation data — both are observed at their current, established scale, not at launch. 10–20% is a defensible planning range for a brand nobody has heard of, broadly consistent with cold B2B outreach benchmarks, but it should be tested, not assumed, before being presented as fact.

07 Sales & Marketing Avenues to Close the Gap

Since Year-1 engagement, not category coverage, is the binding constraint on revenue, the highest-leverage work in Year 1 is marketing and outreach strategy, not further financial modelling. Six concrete avenues, roughly in order of cost-effectiveness:

  1. Consumer-driven nomination, not cold outreach to businesses. Rather than approaching tradespeople cold, target homeowners directly ("nominate your favourite builder") through low-cost social channels. A consumer nominating a business they already trust has a fundamentally different, more favourable response dynamic than a stranger cold-emailing that business — zero downside, positive framing, no sales pitch involved.
  2. Seed with a small number of respected local names first. Before any broad outreach, personally approach a handful of well-regarded local tradespeople in each launch area to be founding nominees. Their credibility legitimises the scheme for everyone who follows.
  3. Distribute through partners who already have the audience. A builders' merchant, trade supplier, or trade body sponsor already has a mailing list and customer relationships an awards programme starting from zero does not. Making early sponsorship partly about co-promotion, not just a logo fee, turns their existing reach into the programme's reach.
  4. Local press and PR, not paid advertising. Regional newspapers regularly cover local business award stories as genuine local-interest content, at no media cost.
  5. Concentrate the launch instead of spreading thin. Running a smaller number of locations and the ~22 high-fill categories first concentrates marketing effort where it can actually build word-of-mouth critical mass, rather than diluting a fixed effort across a much wider footprint.
  6. Lead every piece of messaging with "free to enter." The most likely objection driving low engagement is simply "why would I bother" — a brand-new, unknown scheme needs to make the zero-cost, zero-downside nature of entry the first thing anyone hears.

08 Recommendation: Test Before Presenting a Final Figure

This is the most important recommendation in the report. Every other assumption here is either reasonably evidenced (pricing, market existence) or a bounded, sensible range (event structure). Year-1 engagement is neither — and no further desk research will resolve it.

  • Run a small real pilot before finalising any investor-facing figure: pick 2–3 categories in 2–3 locations, run the actual nomination and outreach process from Section 7, and measure the real response rate.
  • A genuine 20-business test is worth more than further modelling — it replaces the single largest assumption in this report with a real number.
  • Present the range, not a single number, until that test exists. The honest range in this report (£106K–£241K) is a more defensible position to any serious investor than a false-precision figure that doesn't survive the first follow-up question.
